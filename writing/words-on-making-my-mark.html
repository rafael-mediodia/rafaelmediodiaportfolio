Words on Making My Mark

Rafael Mediodia

In my design education, I often felt a sense of hesitation, worrying about what is considered “good design” rather than trusting my own instincts at times. I became rigid, sticking to safe, predictable choices to protect my grades—admittedly a bad reason in art school, but it is what I did. As a result, my work became far more sterile than I wanted and I didn’t feel my voice within the body of work. Senior year, my goal was to reorient myself, and this project is a direct response to that tension between my compliance and curiosity.

I initially used the word play to describe my process, but realized it masked my true interest: drawing. Cas Holman’s Playful outlines how play is a space of deep intent where we can experiment without the anxiety of evaluation. For me, sketching is that zone. It is a generative tool where I build an arsenal of raw ideas that I can eventually deploy through graphic design or whatever form I choose. Reading Paul Soulellis’s ideas on “urgent craft”—prioritizing immediacy over preciousness and keeping formats open—helped me find room for this intuitive fast-paced making within a formal design practice. I ground my work in the friction between two core states: The Mark and The Assembly.

The Mark

The Mark is the immediate physical and digital residue of my thinking, driven by the power of sketching. This lives in my interests where letters communicate as images and images communicate like words. I value these marks for their roughness. There is an honesty of a human glitch and the visible evidence of hidden labor that proves a person was there making something happen.

The Assembly

The Assembly refers to the act of gathering, sequencing, and tiling these marks into new systems. This is how I allow myself to play make-believe with a grid using the techniques of graphic design to elevate my doodles. Some of my sketches get built out into font files, some might be used for logos in the future, some might just be published online. I just enjoy how my doodles translate to the screen and maybe back onto paper.

For a long time, I felt a disconnect between my love for doodling and the rigid expectations of traditional typography. I turned to type design as a way to reconcile that relationship, discovering a method to translate my drawn forms directly into functional systems. I feel that I am more attuned to typography’s forms through these explorations (and Richard Lipton’s lovely type design class). I value the inherent neatness of a system more so now, especially for what it allows me to break.

This willingness to look for the translation of the mark across different systems is also how I approach working with others. My practice of building type and texture extends naturally into collaboration, allowing my arsenal of ideas to be tested through different mediums. I collaborated with my freshman year roommate/friend/talented textile designer Ethan Lam on a jacquard weaving where we got to translate our queer online asian worlds onto a woven substrate. I also created title cards and acted for another lovely friend/talented filmmaker Nora Guttman’s thesis film, Providenceland, where I was able to understand how the movement of a body is a way to make marks through time-based media. In both instances, the collaboration became a new kind of assembly and a way to see how language behaves when it leaves my desk and enters a shared space.

That relentless impulse to accumulate and question things goes back to childhood. In third grade, my teacher nicknamed me ‘Curious George’ because I asked so many questions. Today, I still use that same curiosity to handle the massive abundance of thoughts that gather in my head, channeling that through drawing and the techniques of graphic design.

Ultimately, making my mark is about putting the preciousness in process instead of outcome. I want to carry this approach into my professional practice: trusting the friction of the making, anchoring systems in honest drawing, and practicing a fundamental visual generosity by seeing things through to an outcome.
