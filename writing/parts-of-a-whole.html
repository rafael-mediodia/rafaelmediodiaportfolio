Parts Of A Whole

Rafael Mediodia

A broken mug sits in the middle of the living room floor; a child runs out the house into the street past the metal terracotta-colored gate into the festival. Small, quiet, a misshapen bowl cut, a second-hand t-shirt, basketball shorts. The eyes of others feel heavy as they watch the child wove a path amongst the food stalls decorated in warm blues. Smells of barbecue and bread occupy passerbys' noses. The boy's shadow dances left/right/left/right against the glow of many lanterns affixed to metal fences and wooden rods held by festivalgoers.

His flip flops can only carry him so much further across the cobblestone roads, his foot getting caught on an occasional pebble. Bright signs from billboards and stores flash by as the kid tries and flies away from home. His knees hurt. Tears cascade quietly down his face. The buildings blur and become scarce as his route leads him outwards. They become smaller and smaller turning into residential homes then to gas stations and exit signs. Above, the sky is turning pink and the land gets covered in its dim hue. The floor becomes dirt. The child finds a thrush of grass towards the outskirts of the town off the side of the road that leads into the vast forest that's frightful and freeing. There were rumours about its nature.

Rushing really rapidly, the child chops through the tree branches. Leaves brush on his arms as the forest swallows him. Bits of the sky scarcely made it to the dirt floor. The way in which he remembers where he went was wildly wobbled. The night makes him cold and unsure. There is a small sort of sway in the wind that swallows the worry that wickedly wakes in the child's mind.

Woe is he to be the subject of a crime he did or didn't commit.

Time had passed tumbling. His path running led him down a hill. Legs first, his body danced alongside the slope. Tired toes finally land at the edge of a cave a little ways past the hill's foot. The boy takes in the fresh cold in his nostrils. Curiously, the qualms went away as he sits on a rock near the entrance. Tattered were his sandals. He sits and listens to the lyrics of the echoes that call back to him from the cavern. Should he enter or exit the eerie area?

He follows his curiosity into the crevice, crawling closely towards the walls. It's dark and wet inside. The child's teeth chattering bounces off the walls. They become narrow as the child runs forward, hugging uncomfortably holding the child by his shoulders. The cave was as long as a tantrum. From the deep dark grows a path that leads to somewhere bright and lush. A field full of tall grass and blue mountains in the far distance where it rains soft sand that swirls into structures so extravagant. The sand makes castles with gates and tall spires. The dirt is pillows and the air is sweet with the smell of lavender. It's jovial, the residents are small specks of sparkles that glimmered as they flew on their wings greeting the child. Some walked though, and that's okay. They were able to enjoy the elusive flora that flourished throughout, sparkling the ground in blue, pink, and yellow. The child choked at the beauty, and followed his gut towards the river as the pale teal sky fluttered.

The river sang a tune mellow splashing off the stones, and the child stopped to hear its song. It floated him along onto the other side, not sure how and not sure why. The rocks beneath him glimmered wet with the water reflecting the sky above. The river was wide but not long, looping in circles around an island. The trees greet him at the other side. One tall hill emerged from the surrounding trees. A little pimple on the earth's face.

Sat atop the hill was a faded blue house warm with love from someone before. Two-storied, a path for a vehicle, pants made of brick, a shirt of wood, three hats of tile, five glassy eyes, and a front porch with a rocking chair and a little tree in a pot. The doorknob rattled, and the child greeted himself inside. He visited here in his sleep before. The rooms he roamed changed by the day, but the outside stayed the same. He tried to leave, opening the door, but he rolled into another room. The room had one mug and a rug and a window outside looking towards the fields. He tried his luck again running through the next available exit, but it would lead to another room filled with cups of all sorts. Some were big, some were small. Some had handles, some were meant to be hugged by both hands. He tried at it until he reached a room with a large bed with a quilted blanket in the same hue of blue as the abode.

He lied down beneath the covers, gave up, and closed his eyes.

The Monster

There once was a mysterious monster, the type to hold a twig. A long, long branch that drags on the floor leaving a flowing glow wherever it goes. Two little eyes of white amongst a body of fur and scales. It could be tall, it could be small. A magic mystery for many, a folktale for friends to feel as if there was something more that made them small. It hides amongst the tunnels in the daytime and rides on the roofs during the smoggy nights.

As it roams the evening, it likes to hear the conversation of the river of people passing. The festival is full of fleeing feelings. People argued where to visit, wondering if the tale of the house is true, and where they could rest their legs. Suddenly, it hears something unpleasant. It sees the child run out into the large crowd. It's the type to notice nicely. It wonders while it wanders, following the child from above and below, hiding from strangers, strollers, and streetlights. Why might it be so interested? Through the grass on the outskirts, the monster slithers and wades through the dirt. The kid enters the cave. The creature follows, crawling with its twig in hand. Through the dirt, a path of light is carved.

Tak, tak, tak.

Noises bounce around the stones.

Tak, tak, tak.

It is not hugged by the walls. The light shines into his eyes.

It sees into the luscious land and illusion. The rain creates a village for him to wander around should it be interesting to do so. The monster drags the branch across the soft dirt. Thud, thud, thud. The sand slurs down, a monarchy not present to witness the destruction of its castle. A truth or a lie is revealed in the particles falling, but the monster does not care to find out.

Slowly, it treks past the grass. The monster's curiosity took it across the river. Up the hill, the monster and his branch stand in front of the house. The stick plays a beat on the porch steps. Into the door, they went, then another. Then another. Then another long, long hall. Then another door. Soon, there it was in front of the sleeping one. The bed was surrounded by cups. They line the floor with some areas more concentrated than others. Mugs lay on the bedside table and on neighboring shelves. The branch waves across the floor as the monster drags his body forward. Clang. Crash. The boy remains asleep amongst the commotion. The monster does not care to walk on eggshells.

At the bed, it rests its legs. It keeps watch as the boy dreams.

The Parents

The parents were amorphous and glowed with hope of their own. Two who stayed as a pair taking on the shape of what the world wanted them to be. The pair fearfully flocked to their neighbors. Not here. Not there. The two ran around again and again, their heads spun off their shoulders. Their bodies followed after as they rushed relentlessly. How did their heads know where their bodies went, and where did the bodies run without the heads? The festival sings in the rhythm of the bouncing bodies with drums, bells, and choirs singing,

The House! The House! Oh, may the House return!

Children ran around a blue shrine with roofs on their heads attached by a string. Priests wearing dark blue held still amongst the movement of the crowd.

Everything screwed back on together once they saw something glimmering lead into leaves. They have run far from the festival. Sweat beads down their torsos carrying the glitter. Worried whispers were shared until they saw the trail slowly fade generating a push behind their backs. They trembled and tripped over the roots and rocks. Woe are they to lose their child amongst the madness? Mopey faced they went. What a cave it was! It could just swallow you up, couldn't it?

The walls scraped against their skin as they flossed through the cave's teeth. The sweat on their clothes pushed them further into the abyss, sliding them through like slick snails. Light crowned their heads as they fell to their knees. The place feels almost make-believe. Is this the right place to be? Blades stuck out from the mounds of sand, and mounds of sand stuck out in the field. Tears joined their sweat as the dust greeted their eyes. They roamed around making circles in the sand and dirt until the glow they met before said hello close to the edge of the grass. Solace was short-lived as they encountered the body of water. They took off their shoes and the cold water splashed their legs hitting them with rocks.

Barefooted and tired, they dragged their legs to the summit. The parents knocked with no reply. The knob rattled and the two fell through the front. The wall of rooms put them in a befuddled state. Every room they entered felt like the one prior. Until, it wasn't.

They sat down, wrists weak. The room is covered in broken ceramic, fur, and scales. The parents looked from afar to see their child, eyes closed beside a beast. The monster's eyes opened and grabbed the branch that leaned beside the bed.

Hello again.

The parents tiptoed around the remaining mugs fearfully. One misstep and all the mugs begin to shatter. The boy's eyes opened.

At the bed. There they were.

The Return

Crack. The picture frames in the room became bigger in size. Krrrkt. The party of four became closer and closer, the floor shifting below them. The mugs clanged against the walls and the furniture danced with it. Fur melded to skin. Their thoughts began to blend. They understood and held each other as everything caved in. Soon, arms and legs lined the outside of the house. Eyes found their way to the roof. The front door became a mouth. Blood pumped through the plumbing. Anatomy all entangled.

The house soon lifted off the ground as a newborn bird. They crawled down the hill crushing the speckled critters. Their feet sloshed through the river making waves watering the flowers before crushing them. Mud and sand accumulated on their legs as they spread across the field. The windows saw a shining red in the sky as they galloped onward.

The cave passage did not matter any more for their combined strength shattered the walls carving a path. Trees stood no chance as they left the cave; cars stood no chance as they trekked the road; buildings stood no chance under the strength of their feet. The ground continued to shake in the tempo of something solemn.

The tip of the house was seen above the city walls. At first, they were greeted with screams. Then, their ears protruding from the windows heard the people of the city cheering wildly. Horns blared announcing the return from guards in towers lined along the gates. The festival now surrounded the house. People plotted ways to climb up the sides and dance with them like little bugs on something sweet. Drums and bells were rung from the porch leaving the collective confused. Parents carrying their kids pointed and smiled at the chaos and asked if they would like to join as well. Men in robes ran around the legs with rope as they made their way to the center of the festival causing them to trip over themself. One arm continued to drag from the backdoor.

Tak, tak, tak.

The path shown taken to tomb.

Soon, the house was laid down in the center of a circle, a bonfire. The last sight was a crowd sideways cheering. They laughed. People ripped at their legs and arms and grew big in their bellies. Woodchips stuck between their teeth. A fire engulfed the home. Fragments of metal and bones fell off their sides. It smells like sulfur. The ash flew over the town and into the forest. It was warm and a new start.
