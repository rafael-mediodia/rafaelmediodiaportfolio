Little Bits of Odd

Rafael Mediodia

Landing

The Greater Tree was where all happens and has ever happened for all I've known. It grew from the ground; no one knows where it exactly came from other than a large comet that shot from the sky with a trail of bright green, yellow, and orange. On impact, its roots drew down into the core of the planet, sucking out the energy of its further surroundings, growing the tree upwards until no one could tell where the top was. People gravitated towards it for what it could mean for their futures. Magic moments made its way throughout the Earth; bugs grew big and plants of unknown varieties found homes in the soil. Mushrooms that made you honest and sprouts that grew from the unexpected. People gravitated towards it because there was nothing left: homes destroyed, resources drained, and economies depleted. For those desperate, they sent their kids to the sky.

Houses began to hang from the branches and establishments and travel infrastructure grew up from where the roots lay. Small rivers flow through the land that lead to the ocean. A quiet breeze flows and that allows for the residents to float down from the top on most days, but people often complained the ride back up was always a chore.

The kids near the center of the tree learned in the grass that laid far above their heads and a little ways away from the tree. Truths and lies rode between the blades. Once old enough, they were allowed to peer a little above the field.

Tales lay ahead of them.

Morning Ride

"Breakfast's ready."

Slabs of spam, egg, and some rice make its way to the wooden table in the dimly lit kitchen. The light catches onto the plates and the vase of oddly large flowers to a picture frame of a man near an offering of fruit. The two children quietly scarfed down the meal making sure to look towards the tree before they ate.

The kids waited on the curb, kicking the rocks and other random debris at the bug stop. The sound of thousands of feet signal for them to look up.

"Hop on," yells the big bug.

The kids climb the small ladder near its head and make their way to the closest available seat. The walk to their seat on the big bug is always kind of annoying because big bug is quite the long one. The late Great Summer morning breeze keeps the kids cool as big bug gallops to the different neighborhoods in the district. The older of the two stares towards the tree again wondering about another world.

There's nowhere to rest your head, so the kids lean on each other's shoulders.

Things Will Be Okay

A young one peers out of her home with a slight headache.

She does not know who lives beside her or where exactly she is. The bed is a mess, the books are scattered on the ground and somehow the wall as well. It's her first time out in the fields. She goes to the basement to sync the downstraw with the surrounding plumbing; it's always a chore after a storm, but if she didn't do it, there would be no running water.

She puts on her gear, exits the acorn, and treks through the tall grass. The light is a little too bright today causing her to wince throughout the path. The leaves are large, and the mud tracks on her boots leaving sludge where the concrete begins. Who thought of putting sidewalks in large forests was beyond her. She sees the sap on the trees and knows she can take it easy; she's close. The office building was in viewing distance.

She fixes her tie and crosses the creek.

Reunion

Signs of restaurants houses punctuated with flat fields of grass flash by in the same manner the topics of conversation change from who's getting married to a memory of sitting together before first block.

"We're here."

The group walks into the establishment called "Woody's." The chatter is loud, and there is a 15 minute wait to be seated, but that's okay because they haven't seen each other in a while.

"Should we sit at the side or the corner?"

The group gathers around the left corner at the back. In front of them are five slabs of wood with writing. The menu is comforting: burgers, pasta, fries, with specialties like stone soup and three-leaf clover. They scan the list, putting it down because the wood they're using is weirdly heavy.

Will you come back soon?

I don't know just yet.

Hello, are you ready to order?

Yeah, I'll take the grassfolds and the truth.

I'll just have a milkshake and the truth.

I'll have what she's having.

Hm… I'll have the clover special and what's honest.

I'll be right back.

Thank you. Thanks. Thanks. Thanks.

Do you think it will be the right place for you?

Not sure yet.

Do you want to leave?

Kind of, but there's a charm about here, no?

The waiter returns. Everyone's kind of happy, and kind of sad somehow too.

Commandments

A hum of children half-whispering through the corridors in a single file line causes the teacher to put her fingers to her lips. Their voices quiet as they walk in through the back and dipped their hands into the bowl of water wiping it on their bodies.

Their hands are now dry as they get seated; one child's hands begin to pool. The haze begins to form; chants and songs are sung by the crowd of children. A feeling of discomfort joins the young one's stomach along with a cracker and a bit of sap. Everything gets bright; the man yells; the kids cheer; books close; it falls silent; everyone is now seated.

The session ends and we all head through the exit. "Here's yours," and a large leaf is placed in their arms. "Carry it always."

Throughout the day, some people forget the leaf's presence, some hide it in different places: between the floorboards, the cabinets, and in their bags. The young child breaks it up into pieces and eats bits and pieces of it.

The clock strikes two, and the kids line up to the boats.

Seen

The heat was miserable yet the surroundings were comforting, lesser palm trees and mountains far off in the distance and electrical lines that were way too close to the buildings around it. The sounds of crickets filled the nighttime air and vehicles that swung from the artificial branches created winds that flowed through the buildings. It's grandmother's birthday, and you take a little machine bug to the family house. Last time you went, they only used the real bugs. It seems the family has noticed you've changed a lot too: You've grown so tall! you've grown so wide! You look like him, he looks like you!

You see your grandmother sitting in her bed. She can barely hear. Barely see, even.

You remember the last time you were here. She sat you with your cousins, all girls, and told you about when the Greater Tree appeared. How she had to hide as the earth shaked. She talks about her husband and how she was able to have the life she had because of him. She tells you "Your husband is your degree" which is an antiquated thing to say in the first place, but you notice she looks right at you. The glance you share with her lasts in a way that feels almost like a nod.

Odd Ride

Perfect.

The barista sat on the glowing bench at The Park. The area was peaceful; it was close to the Grotto but not too far from the Outer Ring. Sneaked a sandwich too, haha. The break was only fifteen minutes, but he'd prefer to be in nature than in the middle of the rush hour. It was nice seeing people hanging out together. A family of five were having a picnic with their bug, a couple was sharing a tender moment on the lilypads, and a group of around twenty were digging a hole. He sits and enjoys his sandwich until he feels something odd in his stomach.

The ground begins to shake and the bench starts to float into the air, prompting the barista to jump off onto the nearby grass. What the hell. It begins to rotate profusely and the nearby surroundings begin to glow as some sort of response and the bench floats over to the nearby hole. It then buries itself deep, deep inside. A large sprout grows from the ground. There's a collection of cheers throughout The Park. The mayor is even there. Everyone is cheering, and dancing too. It's a little unnerving really. The barista picks himself off the ground, and runs like the wind towards the noise. The group of twenty have begun to hold onto the branches. The barista rests his behind on the closest branch. The plant soars up towards the big tree passing through swarms of birds and bees and some close calls with planes and satellites. As the party of twenty-one soar upward, the plants' roots disappear into dust that scattered across the park.

The barista begins to think of home and jumps off the branch to then land on soft ground.

Dream Trip

The flight is going to take forever. The family sits together with helmets in their hands. There's a great wild gust on the horizon that has yet to cool down. It's different when it's windy. The Greater Tree's branches shake in ways that create what's unknown. That was sort of the charm until you can't keep your footing. The littlest one asks if they'll be okay, it's his first time on the big bird.

Look, glitter!

The lights turn off. A small dream begins. Sit up straight and look right. Will you see before again? Will it be the same? The questions shook out the branches and through the windows. The eldest leans on his mother's shoulders.

Thank you for the journey.

Reminder

A tattered room with white walls and wooden floors beaten by many feet of the past and roots grown underneath. A silent whirr of the heating system fills the absence of fellow humans in the room, but you could still hear the pitter patter of footsteps from above. This building is way older than the average person alive—or at least the ones inside of it right now.

In the corner sits a curious figure with a brush in hand. His professor tasked the class to draw something comforting. Comforting, he thought, that could be anything at this point. He moved his forearm across the canvas.

The colors blended, crashed, and settled to a cold grey. Comforting? It had been hours at that point, so he decided to take a break and check his phone.

Miss you. It's been lonely without you here. Remember when you were this small?

The message followed with a picture. Him in the snow.

Old Friend

It loved tending to the animals in the farm with the boy when they were younger. It would help count out how many animals have passed through the gates, tallying how many left would need to return from the pasture. After their final tally, they would go and collect from the nearby garden: carrots, tomatoes, cabbage sometimes getting icked out by the little creatures that were crawling on the produce. After all that hard labor, the pair would return to the front porch to share a cold drink of lemonade.

Isn't this the best?

It sure is.

He took out a small pocket knife and engraved something small in the steps. It looked around his shoulder and smiled.

*-*-

The boy was now a man who returned back to his family's farm home for the summer to show the kids where he had spent his childhood. The large centipede sat on the dry ground and his daughters ran out and immediately stopped as they looked down.

Who's that?

He was confused until he saw the faded inscription. He hadn't thought of it in ages. A faint smile grew under his mustache.

Follow me kids.

They turned away from the house into the wide courtyard. The kids looked in awe at the large stone cocoon in the center surrounded by flowers. Judith hides behind her father as Ella continues curious towards it.

Hello?

The cocoon begins to shake.

Goodbye.

The girl was all alone. She was launched into orbit at a very young age. It was a small hopeful pod filled with the resources she needed.

  • A watch for time.
  • A telescope.
  • A goat.

On her way, she would stare at the stars wondering where she would land and if she could breathe. The pod landed amongst a series of mountains. It was beautiful: the mountains, the wild grass, the irregularity of it all. Two birds sang close to her with each passing morning. A goat scaled the mountain facing her window at noon. She knew this because she had her watch. Her goat also noticed.

The goat was born at the same time the girl was born. It knew nothing other than the curated environment it was brought up in. Calm streets with centipede carriages. A large sprout at the city centre. The mayor was always saying something funny there, but the goat always enjoyed listening as the girl rolled her eyes in the stroller. This billie was really silly. The way in which the world worked had it and the girl hand in hoof. Resources were dwindling and there was no way out but up. Up, up, up.

Only a few were selected at a time. Those who believed in something bigger, or those who willed for a different story for themselves. The tree was all they knew and it was a chance for something more. A kid and their calf were the first ones out. The bungee cord was already taut and the family said their goodbyes. They didn't even get to see over the grass.

I hope they have grass there.

The kid became a speck and a memory once they let go.

The girl sat in bed, looking over at the goat staring out the window. The goat did not dare to leave, but in her eyes there was a curiosity. The girl could feel it. Do you want to go? The goat did not reply, because it was a goat. Only a faint glance.

The girl didn't want to leave home. Her parents left their home. It was a practice of uprooting. Hello was always close to a goodbye. Everything changes. But she knew she must go for it was a great honor to be chosen even if she wasn't fully sure why. Cousins, aunts, uncles. They all gathered and whispered amongst themselves, how lucky! How scary! She'll be fine.

She rose from her bed, looked at the clock on her wrist, and spoke.

It's time?

The goat could only stare and bleat. She touched the padlock, and the window swung open. Hello.
