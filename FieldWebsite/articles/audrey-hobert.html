what's new with audrey hobert?

A former Nickelodeon screenwriter making semi-fictional self-portraits, with every track treated like a short film.

At 26, singer-songwriter Audrey Hobert has already lived several creative lives. Before her 2025 debut, she spent years behind the scenes, co-writing for artists like Gracie Abrams while completing a degree in screenwriting at NYU. That cinematic sensibility now permeates every corner of her music: each song feels less like a track and more like a scene pulled from a film only she could direct.

Her debut album, Who's the Clown?, released via RCA Records, arrived with the kind of fully-formed visual identity rarely seen from a first-time artist. Hobert describes her songwriting style as a "talk-singy inner monologue," confessional, slightly unhinged, and deeply funny. The album's title track sets the tone, a meditation on performance, persona, and the terrifying question of who's actually in control.

Drawing from a visual palette influenced by Greta Gerwig, Sofia Coppola, and the chaotic charm of 2000s Disney Channel stars, Hobert treats every track like a short film. The prosthetic imagery in her music videos isn't shock for shock's sake but a deliberate challenge to the polished aesthetics of contemporary pop.

Key tracks from the album:

  • "Sue Me" is the breakout viral single that introduced Hobert's talk-sing cadence to a wider audience.
  • "Bowling Alley" is a cinematic slow-burn with what Hobert calls "sad clown energy."
  • "Wet Hair" is a nostalgic anthem about the mundane moments that linger longest.
